# Archivexus Vision

> **Every world has a memory. Archivexus preserves it.**

---

# What is Archivexus?

Archivexus is a knowledge layer for tabletop roleplaying worlds.

Built on top of Foundry VTT, it transforms isolated documents into a connected, living memory that grows alongside your campaign.

Actors, Journals, Scenes, Items, Maps, Organizations, Events, and every other piece of your world become part of a single interconnected network of knowledge.

Archivexus does not replace Foundry.

It helps Foundry remember.

---

# Vision

Every campaign becomes larger than anyone can remember.

Names are forgotten.

Relationships become unclear.

Events lose context.

Notes become duplicated.

Knowledge becomes fragmented.

The Game Master should never have to fight against their own documentation.

Archivexus exists to preserve the memory of a world.

Not only by storing information,
but by understanding how every piece of that world connects to every other.

The Game Master tells the story.

Archivexus remembers it.

---

# Design Philosophy

## Memory over Documentation

Foundry stores documents.

Archivexus preserves memories.

Documents are containers.

Memories are connected.

Archivexus models the world behind the documents.

---

## Knowledge through Relationships

Knowledge is not defined by isolated notes.

It is defined by connections.

Characters belong to places.

Places belong to regions.

Organizations recruit members.

Events change the world.

Stories emerge from relationships.

Archivexus understands those relationships instead of treating them as simple links.

---

## A Single Source of Truth

Every piece of knowledge should exist only once.

Different views, maps, graphs, timelines, and documents should simply represent the same underlying information.

Updating one place should update every representation automatically.

---

## History is Part of the World

Campaigns evolve.

Characters move.

Kingdoms rise and fall.

Secrets are revealed.

Nothing should be lost.

Archivexus preserves the evolution of a world instead of replacing its past.

The history of the world is just as important as its present.

---

## Optional Structure

Every Game Master works differently.

Some prefer writing.

Others prefer structured data.

Neither approach is wrong.

Archivexus should provide structure when it is useful, never when it is mandatory.

---

## Automation over Maintenance

The user creates.

Archivexus connects.

The user writes.

Archivexus organizes.

The user changes the world.

Archivexus updates its memory.

Automation should remove repetitive work, never creativity.

---

## Transparency

Your knowledge belongs to you.

Archivexus should never hide information behind proprietary formats.

If Archivexus disappears, your Foundry world should continue to exist.

Archivexus enhances Foundry.

It never owns your data.

---

## Extensible by Design

Archivexus should be a platform rather than a collection of features.

New block types.

New relationships.

New visualizations.

New tools.

Everything should be expandable without changing the foundation.

---

# Long-Term Goal

To become the memory layer for tabletop roleplaying.

Not by replacing existing tools.

But by connecting them.

---

# Guiding Question

Every new feature should answer one simple question:

> **Does this help preserve the memory of the world?**

If the answer is yes,
it probably belongs in Archivexus.

If the answer is no,
it probably belongs somewhere else.
